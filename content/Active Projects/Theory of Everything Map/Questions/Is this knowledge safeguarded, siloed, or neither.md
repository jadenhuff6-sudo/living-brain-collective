# Is this knowledge safeguarded, siloed, or neither?

The founder's premise (2026-10-01): "the biggest reason why we haven't unlocked a lot of this information is it's been safeguarded, one, but it's also been siloed and not communicating back and forth." This page tests that premise instead of assuming it, using the pilot's checked claims.

## The explanations being compared

- **H1 Safeguarded:** records exist but are kept secret by the people who hold them.
- **H2 Siloed:** records are public or partly public, but scattered across archives, languages and databases, so nobody connects them.
- **H3 Neither (mundane):** the records say less than they seem to; there is little hidden to unlock.
- **H4 Both:** some records were safeguarded first and then released into silos.

## Reading (pilot size, not a verdict)

Counting inconsistencies, the way the method asks: H1 has 1, H2 has 0, H3 has 3, H4 has 0. **"Both" (H4) and "siloed" (H2) fit the pilot's evidence best**, and the plain "neither" reading fits worst. That is about nine pieces of evidence, so treat it as a direction, not a finding.

## Predictions that would tell them apart

- If H1 is driving it, Mandatory Declassification Review requests for the 1976 protocol's companion files should come back redacted or denied.
- If H2 is driving it, the State Department telegrams on the protocol (already public at the National Archives) should contain details nobody has linked to the 2022 publication.
- If H3 is true, a clean read of the 1976 page 6 scan and the 1971 Australian paper should add nothing beyond false-alarm housekeeping.
