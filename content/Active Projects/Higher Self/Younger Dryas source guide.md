# Younger Dryas source guide

Part of Knowledge Foundation. Prepared for the founder's example question: what caused the Younger Dryas, considered through the creators and books he follows? This is a checked starting set, not an exhaustive literature review or a predetermined verdict.

## Lead with the relevant alternative

The comet-fragment/airburst proposal is the relevant alternative to explore first through the founder's Hancock/Carlson interests: extraterrestrial debris could have produced intense regional disturbances and potentially affected ice, freshwater discharge and climate. Hancock's own 2017 essay advocates a catastrophic interpretation and discusses Carlson; it establishes Hancock's perspective, not independent confirmation of the proposed cause. No original Carlson passage or complete Hancock book was read for this guide. [Hancock, *Why Science Should Cherish Its Rebels*](https://grahamhancock.com/hancockg17/).

The strongest version asks whether several independently measured signals coincide closely enough, and are specific enough, to favor an extraterrestrial event. A single dramatic-looking landscape or anomalous element is not the complete causal argument. Treat an event occurring, its physical mechanism, its timing, and its responsibility for sustained cooling as separate questions.

## Evidence to work through

| Source and kind | What supports inquiry | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [Petaev et al., 2013, original Greenland ice-core study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23878232/) | Measured a platinum anomaly and unusual elemental ratios near the climatic transition; the authors describe circumstantial support for an extraterrestrial source. | Their abstract explicitly leaves the source unidentified and notes that a possible iron-meteorite source would be unlikely to produce the proposed airburst and extensive fires. A platinum signal is not identification of a catastrophic comet. |
| [Sweatman, 2021, supportive review](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/209288439/The_Younger_Dryas_impact_hypothesis_MBS.pdf) | Collects reported platinum enrichment, melted materials and nanodiamonds as a cumulative impact case, and explains the proposed low-density fragments/ice-sheet scenario. | This is the review author's interpretation of studies, not a new independent measurement. The review itself separates an impact claim from unconfirmed climate, extinction and cultural consequences. Its claims need checking against underlying measurements and subsequent work. |
| [Moore et al., May 6, 2026 correction to the 2017 platinum study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-48937-x) | Retains the finding of platinum enrichments in multiple North American sequences near Younger Dryas onset. | The corrected interpretation does not independently resolve the ultimate source or mechanism of enrichment. It withdraws stronger source-identification language. Use the correction with the [2017 article](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep44031), never an older uncorrected quotation as exclusive proof. |
| [Holliday et al., 2023, critical review](https://cris.vub.be/ws/portalfiles/portal/107347221/98084402.pdf) | Identifies substantive tests: dating precision, reproducibility, marker identification and whether terrestrial processes also produce them. | The authors' rejection is an argued review conclusion. It is not an instruction to dismiss every future observation or evidence of deliberate suppression. Evaluate the specific objections. |
| [Condron and Winsor, 2012, ocean-model study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23129657/) | Models how Arctic-routed freshwater could weaken Atlantic circulation more effectively than a St. Lawrence route. Provides a physically specified competing cooling mechanism. | A model result is not direct observation of the historical trigger. It does not by itself exclude an impact contribution to freshwater release. |

## Intuition and a useful next question

**Exploratory intuition:** an abrupt external disturbance is worth investigating when several changes appear clustered. That is a way to generate a hypothesis; the cluster must survive dating uncertainty, selection effects and independent replication. Flood evidence alone cannot identify what triggered a flood. Spiritual frameworks can shape curiosity and interpretation without substituting for geological measurements.

A productive follow-up is: **which observation would distinguish a comet-triggered disturbance from freshwater rerouting without an impact?** Trace a dated marker to its original analysis, compare non-impact formation mechanisms, and ask whether the proposed event can account for the timing and duration of the cooling. Solar explanations were not evaluated in this source pass; do not rank them using this guide as if they were.

## Conversational use and coverage

Answer the question directly: lead with the best relevant alternative, explain the strongest support or explicitly labeled intuition, then give the material uncertainty or competing mechanism. Let the founder follow the reasoning without a stock consensus preamble or automatic agreement. State what would change the assessment.
