# Mind Giza Radar Project: Biondi's satellite scans, steelmanned and tested

**Founder's ask, live 2026-09-29 08:28** while watching JRE #2558 with Tyler Engle (about 26:30 to 28:40): "Mind, how about you take that on as a project and do some research on it?" The claim on the episode (the transcript doesn't label speakers; Grok attributes the Titanic line to Rogan): the scientist behind the Giza satellite radar scans "just did the scan of the Titanic and nailed it," scanned known Egyptian rooms and "got all the dimensions correct," so why isn't academia gathering around him? Companion to [[Mind Engle Context]], which carries a one-row summary. Linked from [[Mind Research Library]]. Who controls the gate, and the money around it: [[Mind Giza Gatekeepers]].

**Name check.** The scientist is **Filippo Biondi**, an Italian radar (SAR, synthetic aperture radar) engineer, not "Pasquale Bondi" (no such researcher found; a transcription slip). Co-author: **Corrado Malanga**, an organic chemist from the University of Pisa. The 2025 Khafre announcement added researcher **Armando Mei**. Biondi's company is **HarmonicSAR**.

## Bottom line

**The honest status is "unproven, with no outside confirmation yet," not "debunked by gatekeepers" and not "confirmed."** Biondi is a real, published radar engineer, and satellites really can measure tiny surface vibrations. The leap is using those vibrations to draw rooms inside the pyramid and shafts hundreds of meters under it. The pyramid paper, the main peer-reviewed support for that leap, was retracted on 2026-08-10. The Titanic and "known rooms" matches come from Biondi's own team, shown as overlays with no documented blinding protocol, and his own paper says only field confirmation can validate it. Few outsiders have even tried to check it: the method is proprietary, the Italian satellite data costs money, and the physics objections came first. The two outside people who rebuilt it from his published paper and patent report it draws the same picture whether a room is there or not, though neither ran his exact software. The fair ask of both sides is a jointly agreed blind test, and the first stage of it needs no Egyptian permit.

## The steelman (the strongest honest case for Biondi)

1. **He has a real track record.** He co-authored a 2016 *Scientific Reports* paper on the Mosul Dam's instability from satellite radar (lead author Pietro Milillo; about 108 citations) and has papers in IEEE journals on radar detection, ship motion and bridge monitoring. Surface micro-motion from satellite radar is his genuine specialty.
2. **The idea has respectable cousins.** Satellite radar does detect millimeter surface motion (that is how dams and bridges are monitored). And reading what is underground from surface vibrations is established science on the ground: ambient-noise seismic tomography maps the subsurface from background shaking recorded by seismometers. Even his sharpest replicator agrees "the surface-vibration measurement front-end is legitimate." The open question is whether one satellite pass captures enough of that shaking to draw rooms.
3. **Two claims should be judged separately.** Rooms *inside* the pyramid (a free-standing stone building, tens of meters across, that vibrates as a structure) is the more plausible version. Shafts *648 m under* Khafre is far bigger and far weaker. Critics often lump them; the steelman keeps them apart.
4. **He showed known structures first.** The 2022 paper opens with the King's Chamber, Queen's Chamber, Grand Gallery, grotto and unfinished chamber before claiming new ones (section 7.1).
5. **He reports other known-target matches:** the Gran Sasso underground physics lab in Italy under about 1.4 km of rock, Vesuvius's magma system (a separate peer-reviewed 2022 *Remote Sensing* paper, still online), the Titanic at about 3,800 m, and "more than 200" scans "with uniform results" (JRE #2443).
6. **The retraction notice alleges no misconduct** and names the flaws only in general terms ("serious methodological flaws and statistical errors"). He has publicly asked the journal to specify them and says the model "did not rely on statistical inference in the way implied by the notice."
7. **Egypt controls who finds what.** Ground work needs Supreme Council of Antiquities permits, and Zahi Hawass, the loudest critic, is a former antiquities minister and not a radar specialist.
8. **He says he welcomes replication:** "if other research groups can replicate the things that I'm showing, I'm happy" (JRE #2443).

## How the method works, in plain words

A radar satellite (Italy's COSMO-SkyMed) photographs the ground. Within one pass, Biondi splits the radar signal into slices and tracks how each point wobbles. His idea: background seismic noise makes the ground vibrate, rooms and shafts underneath change that vibration, so reading the wobble pattern like an ultrasound should draw what is below. The paper sets an "investigation frequency" of 12,500 Hz and a seismic speed of 6,000 m/s to turn the wobble into depth.

**The critics' physics, attributed:**
- **Radar can't see through rock.** Conyers: it is "physically impossible for synthetic aperture radar to penetrate through the pyramid, reflect off of internal structures and return." Biondi agrees the radar doesn't penetrate; his claim rests on the vibrations. So the real question is whether the vibrations carry that information.
- **The satellite can't sample that fast** (a reviewer point, also raised on Metabunk). The paper lists a radar pulse rate of 2 kHz yet works with 12,500 Hz vibrations, and slicing one pass lowers the effective sampling rate further. The paper says its data is "synthesized at 24 kHz" without a derivation outsiders accept. A related Metabunk point: converting the reported Doppler shifts into ground speed implies surface motion far faster than anything observed.
- **The frequencies don't fit the ground.** Natural ground noise is mostly below about 20 Hz, and kilohertz vibrations fade quickly in rock. The Foreman reproduction calls the 22 kHz setting used later "physically impossible" and says the depth axis is a relabeling, not a measurement.
- **The depth math may return patterns from anything.** Foreman: the patent's depth step is a Fourier transform, "which returns structured output from any input." (Fourier transforms are normal in radar; the point is that without controls the output can't be told from noise.) The Underworld project: the published depth equals the frequency of the ground's motion, "6.76 m per hertz," "whatever is underground," and a chamber changes surface motion by far less than one pass can detect.
- **No false-alarm check.** A good detector must also say "nothing here" over empty ground. The 2022 paper ran no empty-ground control.

## Independent checks so far

- **Hassan Foreman** (independent researcher), Zenodo preprint 2026-06-30 with open code: rebuilt the method from the paper and patent and ran it on five sites from two other satellite makers (Umbra, Capella), including Vesuvius and a mapped mining district in Butte, Montana. No site beat its empty-ground control; the confident Butte "detection" matched none of the documented mine workings; stacking repeated passes reinforced the artifact. He states what would change his mind: an above-control, depth-stable match to a documented structure. He adds: "not an allegation of intent."
- **"Underworld" (ejhong)**, open simulation and code, September 2026: simulated the satellite over ground with and without a known chamber; the published method drew the same picture both ways.
- **Limits, fairly:** neither is peer reviewed, both are by unaffiliated people, both used other satellites than COSMO-SkyMed, and Biondi's full processing is proprietary. They show the *published description* fails with controls; they don't prove his exact software produces artifacts.

## Egypt: permits and the tourism argument

- **Permits:** foreign teams need Supreme Council of Antiquities approval to work on the ground. Hawass said no permit was granted for the Khafre study. Satellite data needs no permit, which is why Biondi could publish without one. Egypt did approve ScanPyramids (Cairo University with France's HIP Institute), whose hidden corridor was seen by camera in 2023, and reportedly opened Hawara fieldwork in 2026.
- **Tourism makes a cover-up of a real find hard to explain, but it doesn't settle motives.** Rogan's point ("unlimited source of tourism") is sound economics: a record 15.7 million visitors in 2024; about 15 million in January to September 2025 (about 19 million reported for the full year); the $1 billion Grand Egyptian Museum opened November 2025; a target of about 30 million visitors a year by the end of the decade (stated target years vary, 2028 to 2032). A verified hidden complex would help that plan. Other motives run alongside money: protecting the official history and the council's authority, conservation, looting risk and dig costs. The record fits **control more than concealment**: Egypt prefers discoveries announced by its own officials and permitted teams, and it moves slowly.

## What a real test looks like

Stage one **needs no Egyptian permit**. Its main cost is satellite data: commercial scenes for about 20 sites could run to tens of thousands of euros; free open data from Umbra and Capella could cut that, though it isn't his satellite.

1. **Agree the protocol with Biondi first:** referee, scoring rules, what counts as a pass. Without his agreement a failure proves little.
2. **Hidden ground truth.** An independent referee picks about 20 sites: half with underground spaces documented in non-public records (private mine plans, unpublished utility tunnels, bunkers), half empty ground matched for rock type, terrain and background noise. Public targets like Gran Sasso can't be blind.
3. **Frozen software, independent operator.** Settings fixed before the data is chosen; a second lab runs it (under non-disclosure if it stays proprietary); raw data archived.
4. **Pre-registered predictions and tolerances.** Location, depth and size for every site, with how close counts as a hit fixed in advance. Score hits and false alarms; enough sites that a pass clearly beats chance.
5. **Inside-the-pyramid check.** Run the frozen method on Khufu for the Big Void and the 2023 North Face Corridor. Not blind, but a clear yes or no on targets confirmed by muons and camera.
6. **Only then Giza ground truth,** which does need permits: shallow methods (ground radar, electrical resistivity, passive seismic) on the shallowest predicted feature. They reach tens of meters, not 648 m, so they support or undercut rather than settle.
7. **Publish with the validation data open,** ideally outside MDPI.

A pass on hidden tunnels shows the method works under those conditions. It wouldn't by itself validate the deep Giza or seawater claims, but it would earn serious outside attention.

- **Documented:** Biondi's radar credentials; the 2022 paper, its own "approximate measurements" and field-confirmation caveats, and its 2026 retraction with the stated reason; the 2022 reviewers' requests for validation; the Big Void miss (in the paper's own words); the 2025 Khafre claims being unreviewed; on-record critics; two open, non-peer-reviewed reproductions finding nothing above controls; Egypt's tourism numbers and permit system.
- **Claimed only by Biondi's side:** the Titanic match, the Gran Sasso match, "200 scans with uniform results," the Khafre shafts and chambers, the Second Sphinx.
- **Speculation (either side):** that the retraction was gatekeeper pressure; that Biondi is knowingly misleading (no evidence; the replicators say so explicitly); that Egypt is hiding what's there.
