# AI cross-check challenge: what public data alone shows about UAP

**The ask (live, 2026-10-01 08:24):** Grusch on JRE 2560 said AI could "disclose before the federal government" by cross-checking datasets. Founder: "That's my challenge to y'all. How much y'all figure out?" Rule: public and declassified sources only. Each link below is labeled **DOCUMENTED** (a primary record shows it), **STATISTICAL** (a measured association, not a cause), or **SPECULATION** (inference or testimony content with no supporting record). A hearing or interview *documents that someone said it*; what they said stays SPECULATION until a record backs it.

## Spoken summary (3 lines)

1. Grusch's "declassified annex with codes" is real: a 1976 US-Soviet protocol, declassified in 2013, most likely gives code "ATOM 222" for unidentified objects on missile warning systems, but it was built for nuclear false alarms and nothing in it says UFO.
2. The best statistical lead, 1950s sky-plate flashes clustering around nuclear tests, is still a fight: one team finds it, independent re-checks in 2026 mostly do not.
3. Nuclear bases show up again and again in documented incidents, but every record stops at "something happened"; the non-human link is always testimony, and our own check of 80,000 sighting reports found no signal near missile bases once population is counted.

## Bottom line

Cross-referencing public data **does not establish non-human presence.** It does establish three real things: both superpowers wrote a formal procedure for unidentified objects on nuclear warning systems; nuclear sites recur in the documented incident record; and there is one contested statistical anomaly in 1950s photographic plates. That is a better answer to his challenge than hype: an AI can find the paper trail fast, and it can also show exactly where the paper trail ends.

## 1. The 1971 treaty and the "annex with codes" (strongest finding)

- **DOCUMENTED.** Article 3 of the 30 Sep 1971 Accidents Measures Agreement: the parties "notify each other immediately in the event of detection by missile warning systems of unidentified objects, or in the event of signs of interference with these systems... **if such occurrences could create a risk of outbreak of nuclear war**." The last clause matters: it covers only detections that could start a war. [Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/sov001.asp) · [State Dept](https://2009-2017.state.gov/t/isn/4692.htm)
- **DOCUMENTED.** The "2013 declassified annex" Grusch described exists, but it is the **30 Sep 1976 Protocol** implementing the agreement (effective 30 Mar 1977), inside a Secretary of Defense memo to the national security adviser. Carter Library file NLC-12-17-9-2-9 is stamped "No Objection To Declassification in Full 2013 11 15." The National Security Archive (GWU) posted it 24 Mar 2022. It sets "pre-agreed short messages consisting of a code word and numerical designator." [Document page](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/27622-document-3-secretary-defense-assistant-president-national-security-affairs) · [OCR, checked directly](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/27622/ocr) · [Briefing book](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/nuclear-vault/2022-03-24/colonel-odoms-chilling-four-am-phone-call)
  - **ATOM 222** = unidentified objects detected by missile warning systems. (The OCR around 222 is scrambled, but it sits with the "objects... could create a risk of outbreak of nuclear war" message and the messages run in Article 3's order, so the pairing is very likely, not letter-clean.) **ATOM 333** = interference with warning systems or their communications. **ATOM 444** = a failed missile launch that may cross borders. **ATOM 555 / 666** = unexplained nuclear incidents. (111, the accidental detonation message, is unreadable in the OCR.)
  - **The code word is ATOM, not ADAM** in the OCR (ATOM 333, 444, 555 all read cleanly). "ADAM" is most likely a mishearing. Nobody has checked the page image by eye yet.
- **DOCUMENTED context.** Kissinger described the clause to China's ambassador as notice "in the event of detecting unidentified objects if such occurrences could create the risk of nuclear war." [FRUS 1969-76 vol. XIII, doc 17](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve13/d17). "Unidentified objects" was ordinary early-warning language for unknown radar tracks; in 1960 the Thule radar read the moon rising as a missile attack.
- **SPECULATION.** That the clause was written with UFOs in mind. No reviewed document says so. We did not find the negotiating record that explains the wording (gap).

**What this does and does not show:** a contingency procedure existed; it is not evidence that any qualifying encounter happened.

## 2. The 1950s sky-plate flashes and nuclear tests (best statistical lead, contested)

- **STATISTICAL.** Bruehl & Villarroel, *Scientific Reports*, 20 Oct 2025 ([doi 10.1038/s41598-025-21620-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-21620-3)): about 107,000 VASCO "transients" on Palomar POSS-I plates, 1949 to 1957. Transients were about 45% more likely within a day of an above-ground nuclear test (relative risk 1.45, p about 0.008); the widely quoted "68% the day after" is the narrower window. About +8.5% transients for each extra UFOCAT sighting report on a date. The authors claim association only.
- **STATISTICAL.** Solano, Villarroel et al., *PASP* 2025: a deficit of transients inside Earth's shadow at geosynchronous height, read by the authors as possible reflective objects in orbit before Sputnik (SPECULATION as an interpretation).
- **Against it (strength ladder):**
  - Peer reviewed: Hambly & Blair 2024 put the glass plates under a microscope in Edinburgh; the tested transients look like emulsion flaws. **DOCUMENTED** for those objects.
  - Accepted (PASA): Watters et al. 2026, from Harvard's Galileo Project (a UAP-friendly group), says the nuclear link is "almost completely determined by the observation schedule," finds no shadow deficit, and says the catalog mixes in stars, scan artifacts and defects. [arXiv 2601.21946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21946)
  - Preprint: Hayes 2026 rebuilt the catalog with an independent pipeline: +1 day relative risk 1.35, **not significant** (p = 0.17); a model that accounts for nightly plate coverage gives **1.03, p = 0.71**, i.e. nothing. [arXiv 2604.04810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04810) (checked directly)
  - Ordinary explanation not yet ruled out: fallout fogging film is a documented 1940s effect (Kodak traced fogged film to Trinity fallout), which would also tie plate defects to test dates.
- **For it:** authors' reply [arXiv 2602.15171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15171) (says ensemble statistics survive); Doherty 2026 preprint, same catalog, rate ratio 1.80, p = 0.006 [arXiv 2604.00056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00056); Cann 2026 preprint finds detections fall during geomagnetic storms and keeps a 3.1 sigma nuclear link [arXiv 2604.04950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04950). Yang & Villarroel 2026 concede microscopy alone cannot tell real flashes from defects [arXiv 2608.26371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26371).
- **Status:** a disputed association. Re-analyses of the same plates are not independent replications; no other plate archive has tested it.

## 3. Nuclear sites in the incident record (documented events, undocumented cause)

| Case | DOCUMENTED | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| Malmstrom, Echo Flight, 16 Mar 1967 | All 10 Minuteman missiles went No-Go within seconds. Unit history says UFO rumors around Echo "were disproven"; the investigation did not pin the initiating cause; engineers reproduced the shutdown with an electronic noise pulse. [Summary of 2001 FOIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malmstrom_UFO_incident) | UFO over the site = **testimony only** (Robert Salas). The Oscar Flight event (24 Mar) has **no document**; critics say Salas moved his account from Echo to Oscar. A 2025 WSJ report of a secret EMP-test explanation is itself **unverified**. |
| Minot B-52, 24 Oct 1968 | Radarscope film and Blue Book case file exist; the B-52's radio failed during the radar contact. [Case file archive](https://minotb52ufo.com/investigation/investigation-full.php) | Ground-air visual match rests on witnesses. |
| SAC bases, Oct-Nov 1975 | NORAD messages and command director logs (FOIA) record low intrusions over weapons-storage areas at Loring, Wurtsmith, Malmstrom, Minot. [FOIA set](https://minotafs.org/documents/foia-ufo-75.html) | Logs record sightings, not what they were. |
| Rendlesham, Dec 1980 | Halt memo to UK MoD (13 Jan 1981); radiation 0.07 mR/h at the marks vs 0.03-0.04 nearby, near background. [Summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident) | Nuclear weapons at the base never officially confirmed. |
| Los Alamos green fireballs, 1948-49 | Declassified 16 Feb 1949 conference transcript (Teller, Bradbury, LaPaz). Project Twinkle closed as "probably natural." | LaPaz disputed the meteor explanation; no further record. |
| Nuclear sites today | AARO FY2024: 18 drone events near nuclear infrastructure. AARO FY2025 (Jul 2026): 50, "none reported as UAP." *(secondary summaries; primary PDFs not yet read)* | These are drones. |

- **DOCUMENTED (agency assessment):** AARO Historical Record Report Vol. 1 (Mar 2024) found no empirical evidence of off-world technology or a hidden crash-retrieval program. Vol. 2 announced, not found released.
- **Honest framing:** nuclear sites *recur in the selected records*. That is not the same as more incidents than elsewhere: nuclear sites have more radar, more guards and stricter reporting rules, and no comparable non-nuclear denominator exists.

## 4. Our own cross-check (the AI doing it, light compute)

80,331 public NUFORC reports, 1906-2014 ([planetsig/ufo-reports](https://github.com/planetsig/ufo-reports)); test dates from public US and Soviet test lists. Script and output saved at `[local path removed]`.

- **Near missile bases (STATISTICAL, null):** 643 reports within 150 km of the three ICBM bases vs 172 near three same-state comparison towns, but the F.E. Warren circle swallows the Colorado Front Range (about a million people). Malmstrom plus Minot vs Billings plus Bismarck: **122 vs 125, a tie.** Not population-normalized.
- **On nuclear test days, 1951-62 (STATISTICAL, null):** 588 reports. Counting every report: 130 on a test day or the day after vs 102 expected. Counting each date once (removing pile-ups from media flaps): **67 vs 61.6, noise.** Soviet tests: slightly *below* chance.
- **Limits:** NUFORC started in 1974, so 1950s entries are memories filed decades later with fuzzy dates; no season matching; about eight variants tried. Correct wording: **no robust association found in a weak dataset**, not proof there is none.
- **Literature agrees on the pattern:** Medina, Brewer & Kirkpatrick 2023 (*Scientific Reports*; Kirkpatrick is former AARO director) found US sighting reports track sky visibility, dark skies, public land, airports and military installations. [PMC10721628](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10721628). A 2023 study tied a 2020 report surge to Starlink launches.

## Recommendation: the next cross-check worth running

1. **Build a dated, located table from the 2026 war.gov releases, NARA RG 615 transfers and Project Blue Book's case index** (about 12,600 cases dated at the time, about 700 "unknowns"). Blue Book is the right 1950s dataset, not NUFORC. Then test unknowns against nuclear test dates and base locations with season matching and a permutation test. Cheap, fast, all public. **Do this first.**
2. Add the international official sets for base rates: France's GEIPAN (rated cases, about 3% unexplained), UK MoD files at The National Archives, Chile's CEFAA, NASA's 2023 UAP study.
3. Only then attempt a plate replication, and on archives that cover 1951-58 (APPLAUSE / Sonneberg; Harvard DASCH has a gap through most of the test era).

**What would change this assessment:** a primary record linking a nuclear-site incident to a physical object with sensor data, or the plate correlation surviving on an independent archive.
