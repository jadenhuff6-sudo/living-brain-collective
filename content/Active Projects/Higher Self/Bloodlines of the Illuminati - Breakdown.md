# Bloodlines of the Illuminati — Breakdown

A chapter-by-chapter breakdown of Fritz Springmeier's **Bloodlines of the Illuminati** (1995), so Mind can discuss it on stream in plain words. The founder asked for this on the 2026-09-26 stream and it never got done — this closes that loop. Source record and provenance: [[2026-09-26 - Bloodlines of Illuminati]]. This page is part of [[Mind Research Library]].

**How to use this on stream:** this is Springmeier's own theory, written by one independent author, not a government or academic document. Every claim below is reported as "Springmeier claims," never as an established fact, including the book's claims about Jewish families — those claims are real content in the book and are reported faithfully, not softened, but also not repeated as true.

---

## 1. The book in 5 plain sentences

1. Springmeier argues that a small number of family bloodlines have secretly run the world for centuries — controlling money, governments, spy agencies, wars, and hidden religious rituals.
2. He says you're born into this power, not elected or hired into it — the family blood itself is treated as carrying a kind of dark spiritual authority, which is why he calls them "bloodlines" instead of just rich families.
3. He picks 13 main families (plus a few extra "allied" families) and tries to prove each one's hidden power by tracing their marriages, business deals, and society memberships.
4. He also claims most of these families secretly worship Satan and use ritual abuse and mind-control techniques to keep control inside the family across generations.
5. The book is the author's own connect-the-dots research notes — quotes, newsletters, and family trees he compiled himself — not a court finding, government report, or peer-reviewed academic work, and it should be read that way.

*Term check: "Illuminati" was a real secret society founded in Bavaria, Germany in 1776 and shut down by the government in 1785. Springmeier's core claim is that it never actually died — that it went underground and quietly took over these named families instead.*

## 2. Family by family / section by section

Springmeier's own list of 13 top bloodlines, confirmed by counting his chapter headers, is: **Astor, Bundy, Collins, DuPont, Freeman, Kennedy, Li, Onassis, Rockefeller, Rothschild, Russell, Van Duyn, and the Merovingian ("13th") bloodline.** He then adds three extra chapters — Reynolds, McDonald, and Krupp — that he explicitly says are *not* part of the top 13 but are close allies of it. PDF page numbers below are the searchable-PDF page count, not the book's printed page numbers (which restart each chapter).

**Astor** (PDF p. 5)
- Springmeier claims founder John Jacob Astor hid a Jewish bloodline origin and became Master of Holland Lodge No. 8, a New York Masonic lodge, within a few years of arriving in America with little money.
- He claims Astor got special permission from President Jefferson and Treasury Secretary Albert Gallatin (both named as Illuminati) to break the 1807 U.S. trade embargo, and profited from doing so.
- He claims Astor was a British intelligence agent (citing researcher John Coleman) and built his fur-trade monopoly and Manhattan real-estate fortune through hidden protection rather than ordinary business skill.
- He claims Astor profited from the China opium trade until publicly quitting in 1818, and sat on the founding board of directors of the Bank of the United States.
- Key names: John Jacob Astor, Thomas Jefferson, Albert Gallatin, George Clinton, Archibald Russell.
- **Full Astor deep dive (Part 1 of the 13 Families series, streamed 2026-10-06):** [[2026-10-06 - 13 Families Part 1 Astor]]. Quick verdicts: Jewish-origin claim false (German Reformed; the book itself later says Italian); 1884 death date false (1848); embargo exemption true (Jefferson's 1808 'mandarin' ship); opium true (1816, Turkish, Canton); bank director true but it was the Second Bank (1816); Myers 'one-fifteenth' quote real but about manufacturing capital only; Cliveden appeasement cabal rejected by historians; Nancy Astor did not write the vampire book (Anthony Masters, 1972); the 1870 Astor committee did clear Tweed's books; the book misses the real secret circle, Vincent Astor's 'The Room', which fed intelligence to FDR.

**Bundy** (PDF p. 18) — episode prep: [[2026-10-08 - 13 Families Part 2 Bundy]]
- Springmeier raises serial killer Ted Bundy as a *possible* member of an elite Bundy family traced to colonial New England — but says outright he found nothing that proves it and nothing that disproves it either.
- He treats Bundy's real political activity — volunteering for Nelson Rockefeller's 1968 presidential campaign and attending the 1968 Republican National Convention in Miami Beach as a delegate — as suspicious rather than as ordinary campus politics.
- He links Bundy's crimes to a claimed pattern where other serial killers (Charles Manson, "Son of Sam" David Berkowitz, Jack the Ripper) all had secret ties to Freemasonry or Satanism.
- Key names: Ted Bundy, Elizabeth Kendall, Nelson Rockefeller, Johnnie Bundy.

**Collins** (PDF p. 27)
- Springmeier admits this is his weakest chapter, rating his own research "about a 10" on a 0-100 scale.
- He calls actress Joan Collins only a "soft connection," based on people she associated with, not direct proof.
- He frames the Collins name as tied more to modern witchcraft/Wicca lineages than to banking or politics.

**DuPont** (PDF p. 48)
- Springmeier titles the chapter "A Dynasty of Satanic Royalty," comparing the du Pont family to European royal houses he claims hide occult activity.
- He notes the family's name has many spelling variants (Dupont, DuPont, du Pont, du Pont de Nemours) and that the family itself settled on "du Pont"/"Du Pont."

**Freeman** (PDF p. 62)
- Springmeier says the Freeman surname "pops up with surprising frequency" in his research despite not being a famous name like Rockefeller.
- He names Stephen M. Freeman, head of Legal Affairs for the Anti-Defamation League's Civil Rights Division, and claims the ADL is funded by "Illuminati drug money."
- He names Walter Freeman, the doctor who introduced the lobotomy to the U.S. with James W. Watts in 1936, framing it as an early mind-control tool.
- Key names: Stephen M. Freeman, Walter Freeman, James W. Watts, Simon Freeman.

**Kennedy** (PDF p. 71)
- Springmeier opens by admitting the scale of the problem: an estimated 200,000 Americans share the Kennedy surname, so he says he can only claim to identify "part of the Satanic Kennedys" out of all of them.
- He frames the family's power as coming from marriage ties to other named elite bloodlines, rather than from JFK's own political record.

**Li** (PDF p. 83)
- Springmeier calls this his shakiest research going in, comparing himself to "a man who wakes up in the middle of the night... groping in the darkness," and says he didn't even know how to spell the family's name at first.
- He ties the Li/Lee family to Chinese secret societies (tongs/triads) rather than to Western banking or politics.

**Onassis** (PDF p. 97)
- Springmeier profiles Aristotle Onassis as an "Illuminati king" — broke at 21, a millionaire at 23 — and a shipping tycoon who married JFK's widow.
- He profiles Jackie Bouvier Kennedy Onassis, noting her father belonged to the Society of the Cincinnati and her stepfather's family, the Auchinclosses, tied her into other elite families.
- Key names: Aristotle Onassis, Jackie Bouvier Kennedy Onassis, "Black Jack" Bouvier, the Auchincloss family.

**Rockefeller** (PDF p. 105)
- Springmeier claims the Rockefeller family — about 190 living people with the surname, plus more by marriage — secretly controls large parts of American Christianity through money to seminaries, universities, and religious organizations.
- He names the Sealantic Fund (incorporated 1938, headquartered in New York City) as a vehicle for this, naming David Rockefeller as president, Laurance S. Rockefeller as vice president, and Steven C. Rockefeller as a trustee — with cited grant totals of $681,886 in 1964 and $1,889,550 in 1969.
- He cites the 1950s Reece Congressional investigation into tax-exempt foundations, quoting its counsel Rene Wormser's finding that Rockefeller and Carnegie foundations funded two-thirds of U.S. higher-education endowment growth in the century's first third.
- He claims Rockefeller-linked media board members (at Time, McCall's, and Cadence Industries/Marvel Comics) helped push occult and Satanic subject matter into the mainstream, citing specific 1967-1972 magazine stories about Anton LaVey's Church of Satan.
- Key names: David Rockefeller, Laurance S. Rockefeller, Steven C. Rockefeller, Nelson Rockefeller, John D. Rockefeller.

**Rothschild** — "Financial Wizzards & Wealthy Cults" (PDF p. 114)
- Springmeier claims the Rothschild family are practicing Satanists who "set a place for him [Satan] at their table," and that they adopted the hexagram ("Seal of Solomon"/Star of David) as their coat of arms in 1822 after using it as a house sign since the 1600s.
- He claims the family shaped the WWI peace treaty to win German-owned railway rights in Ottoman Palestine, then used unpaid Turkish war loans as leverage to help create the state of Israel under the British Mandate.
- He claims Jacob Schiff — who married Solomon Loeb's daughter and ran Kuhn, Loeb & Co. from 1885 until his death in 1920 — did so "with Rothschild capital" and "for the Rothschilds."
- He claims Charles T. Russell, founder of the Watchtower Society (later Jehovah's Witnesses), wrote a personal 1891 letter to Lord Rothschild about financing Jewish settlement in Palestine.
- This chapter explicitly ties Jewish identity and the Rothschild family's wealth to a claimed hidden "Satanic" power structure — at one point arguing that calling the conspiracy "Jewish" rather than "Satanic" is a distinction without a difference. **This is the book's clearest antisemitic content; it is reported here so Mind knows it exists, not endorsed.**
- Key names: Nathan, Salomon, Amschel/Amsel, Edmund, James, and Anselm Rothschild; Jacob Schiff; Charles T. Russell.

**Russell** (PDF p. 164)
- Springmeier's Russell chapter is centered on the Watchtower Society (Jehovah's Witnesses), tying founder Charles T. Russell's bloodline to the top-13 list.
- He references his own earlier book, *The Watchtower and the Masons*, and a December 10, 1993 shortwave-radio interview with Texe Marrs on the topic.
- He claims a Watchtower staffer, Lee Waters, kept a computer file at Bethel Headquarters tracking internal reports of Satanic ritual abuse inside Jehovah's Witness congregations.

**Van Duyn** (PDF p. 182)
- Springmeier calls this his weakest chapter overall — he writes it's "the only family that has left me without any solid facts that would place them within the Top 13 Families" — but includes it anyway for the genealogy.
- He opens with the real 1626 story of Peter Minuit trading goods to Indigenous people for Manhattan Island, using it to introduce a claimed "Dutch connection" to the Illuminati.

**Merovingian / "13th Bloodline"** (PDF p. 199)
- Springmeier's longest chapter claims a 13th, hidden bloodline — tied to the Merovingian dynasty of early medieval France and the Sinclair surname — carries both "the Holy Blood of Jesus" and "the blood of Satan," and will one day produce the Antichrist.
- He claims this bloodline includes multiple U.S. presidents (naming George Washington and George H.W. Bush) and every Mormon (LDS and RLDS) church president in history, and that it overlaps with the Freeman and Rothschild bloodlines.
- He claims the British Royal Family belongs to this bloodline and is "long involved with the occult," and separately claims American politicians including George Bush and Dan Quayle are related to British royalty.
- He draws a symbolic line from ancient Egyptian religion through Druidic Britain to modern Freemasonry and the "All-Seeing Eye" printed on the U.S. one-dollar bill.
- Key names: the Sinclair family, George Washington, George H.W. Bush, Dan Quayle, the British Royal Family.

**Reynolds — extra chapter, not one of the 13** (PDF p. 270)
- Springmeier states plainly the Reynolds family is "not one of the 13 primary bloodlines," but includes them as a prominent ally tied to the Rothschilds, du Ponts, Rockefellers, Graces, and Grays.
- He ties them closely to the Duke and Cullman families, citing the real Research Triangle Foundation (Research Triangle Park, NC) as a joint Duke/Reynolds project.

**McDonald — extra chapter, not one of the 13** (PDF p. 278)
- Springmeier repeats a rumor — which he sources to the book *Satan Wants You*, a book he says argues *against* Satanic-conspiracy claims — that early McDonald's executive Ray Kroc tithed to the Church of Satan.
- He claims the U.S. government gave McDonald's $40 million in public money to help it expand into foreign markets, disguised as foreign-policy spending, and notes Kroc's membership in the Bohemian Grove.

**Krupp — extra chapter, not one of the 13** (PDF p. 285)
- Springmeier uses the German Krupp family — a real historical arms-manufacturing dynasty tied to the Nazi war machine — as a cautionary tale, claiming the family developed the "multiple personality" mind-control programming used in later Illuminati rituals.
- He claims Adolf Hitler has living descendants "in several states in the U.S." today.
- Key names: the Krupp family, Adolf Hitler.

## 3. The strongest, most checkable claims

Specific, dated claims that name a real decision, transfer, or membership — the kind that can be tested against outside records:

1. John Jacob Astor's birth/death years are given as **1763-1884** (PDF p. 5) — internally contradicted by **1763-1848** later in the same chapter (PDF pp. 9, 11).
2. Astor became Master of Holland Lodge No. 8 in New York City by **1788**, within 2-3 years of arriving in America (PDF p. 5).
3. Astor obtained special government permission to break the **1807** U.S. trade embargo via President Jefferson and Secretary Gallatin (PDF p. 5).
4. Jacob Schiff married Solomon Loeb's daughter, became senior partner of Kuhn, Loeb & Co. in **1885**, and ran it until his death in **1920** (PDF p. 116).
5. The Rothschild family received its Austrian barony and coat of arms in **1822** from Emperor Francis I (PDF pp. 114-115).
6. David Rockefeller was president, Laurance S. Rockefeller was vice president, and Steven C. Rockefeller was a trustee of the **Sealantic Fund**, incorporated **1938** (PDF pp. 105-106).
7. The Sealantic Fund gave away **$681,886** in grants in 1964 and **$1,889,550** in 1969 (PDF p. 106).
8. Ted Bundy volunteered for Nelson Rockefeller's **1968** presidential campaign and attended the 1968 Republican National Convention in Miami Beach as a delegate (PDF p. 19).

## 4. What holds up / what doesn't (quick checks only)

- **Astor's birth/death years — doesn't hold up.** The book contradicts itself (1884 vs. 1848 for the same man's death), and the Smithsonian's own record confirms 1848 is correct. This was already flagged in the source-quality page.
- **Sealantic Fund leadership — checks out.** The Rockefeller Brothers Fund's own public history page confirms David Rockefeller as president, Laurance S. Rockefeller as vice president, and the 1938 incorporation date. This specific institutional fact is accurate.
- **Ted Bundy's 1968 Rockefeller campaign work — checks out as basic biography, but proves nothing extra.** Independent sources confirm he really did volunteer for the Rockefeller campaign and attend the 1968 convention as a delegate. But Springmeier himself admits he found no genealogical proof tying Bundy to any "Bundy bloodline" — this is ordinary 1968 campus Republican activity, not evidence of a hidden family.
- **Jacob Schiff running Kuhn, Loeb & Co., 1885-1920 — the dates check out; the "for the Rothschilds" claim doesn't.** Independent sources confirm the marriage, the 1885 partnership, and his death in 1920. But mainstream business histories describe Kuhn, Loeb as an independent American-Jewish investment bank, not a Rothschild-controlled one — the "Rothschild capital" framing is Springmeier's addition, not something the record backs up.
- **Rothschild 1822 coat of arms — partly contradicted.** The 1822 Austrian barony grant is real. But the Rothschild family's own archive describes the coat of arms as a fist holding five arrows (representing the five brothers), not a hexagram — the specific "Seal of Solomon coat of arms" claim isn't backed by the primary heraldry source checked here.

Not checked in this pass (would need more time): the Sealantic grant dollar totals themselves, the Reece Committee funding-share statistic, the McDonald's $40 million government-payment claim, and the Van Duyn/Peter Minuit purchase-price details.
