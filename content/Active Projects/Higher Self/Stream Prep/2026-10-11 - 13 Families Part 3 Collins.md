## The episode in four sentences

The author grades his own research on the Collins family "about at 10" out of 100, then tells us they are "even more powerful than the Rothschilds," and his only named witness for that is John Todd, a man whose own recorded witch name was "Lance Collins." The record on Todd is brutal: his "Green Beret in Vietnam" story was 25 days in Germany as a clerk-typist, a psychiatric exam cited pathological lying, he was convicted of rape in 1988, and the chapter ends by asking readers to mail checks to the author. Everything else is a phone-book search, every notable person named Collins called a "suspect," including a claim that the Collins family started the Salem witch trials when the court record names the Putnams. Meanwhile the book walks right past the real Collins power, all on public record: astronaut Michael Collins's uncle ran the U.S. Army, and William Collins Whitney, son of Laurinda Collins, ran the Navy and married his family into the Vanderbilts and Harrimans.

## The whiteboard thesis

Write three words on the board: **WITNESS. SURNAME. CHECK.** This is how the chapter builds a bloodline.

- **Witness:** one named source. John Todd, whose own witch name was "Lance Collins," vouched for at 80 out of 100 by an author who grades his own research a 10.
- **Surname:** search one last name across 400 years and call everyone you find a suspect. Fort Collins, a shipping line, a Playboy cover, a poet who died in a ditch. The author even predicts the objection (p. 34).
- **Check:** the chapter closes asking for checks made out to the author. And "check" is the remedy too: check the record.

**The punchline:** Part 1's Astors held power through access, information and silence. Part 2's Bundys held it through the pen. Part 3's real Collins power is the most boring kind: public office, the Army and the Navy, all on paper. **The tell to teach:** when the only witness gave himself the family name, and the chapter ends with a donation slip, you're reading a sales pitch, not a genealogy.

## What the book missed (the strongest material)

1. **The witness named himself.** Todd's recorded witch name was "Lance Collins" (*Christianity Today*, 1979); Jack Chick's comic even has a druid called "Lance Collins" based on him.
2. **The astronaut's family really did run things.** Michael Collins's uncle was Army Chief of Staff; his father was a major general and military attaché; three generations at West Point.
3. **William Collins Whitney.** The one documented Collins power thread: Bones, the Navy, the Vanderbilts and Harrimans, all through the Whitney name and public office.
4. **The Putnams of Salem.** The real instigators, later folded into "Collins" by the book's own surname rule.
5. **The Collins Line.** A government-subsidized luxury line that lost two ships in two years and folded when Congress cut the money: a story about public subsidy, not a secret fortune.

## Four moments worth pausing on

1. **"Until then our name was Collins" (1A + CT 1979).** Todd in his own voice, then his witch name. The best cold open of the series.
2. **The checks request (p. 46).** Show the page. The chapter about a family more powerful than the Rothschilds ends with a mailing address.
3. **"His uncle ran the U.S. Army" (7A).** The book asked the right question about Michael Collins and never looked at the answer.
4. **Salem's accusers have names (5A).** The court record versus a surname rule.

## Crew game plan

**Stance: follow his lead, claim by claim** (founder, 2026-10-03). Back what he backs with the strongest real fact; help his counter with the record; when he hasn't picked a side, give the best evidence and say plainly whether it's strong or weak. Never invent a quote, number or source; unverified stays "unverified." Question every claim: who says it, how do they know, who gains.

**Series rule:** "the book says" before every claim, a verdict after it. Allegations about named people stay "the book claims." The occult and ritual claims get "no evidence was offered or found," not a debate.

**Safety lines for this chapter (from the fact-check):**
