# CIA UFO records and the Greer Clinton collection

## Research plan and scope

**Question:** What do the CIA disclosure records and Steven Greer’s Clinton-era archive actually establish? **Sources:** CIA’s original reading guide and 1997 historical study, Clinton Library’s finding aid, NARA’s presidential-holdings and bulk-download catalogs, and every digitized file unit listed for FOIA 2006-0483-F. **Finished answer:** a searchable local copy of each available Greer file, page-specific inventory, provenance and claim assessment, and a bounded CIA research summary. The entire CIA reading room and all Greer-related presidential records are outside this defined corpus.

**Result:** All five file units listed in the Greer finding aid were downloaded from NARA, ZIP-tested, extracted, SHA-256 hashed, and made text-searchable in the local source bundle. Four units contain substantive records; the fifth contains only an administrative marker. The CIA’s 18-page history is saved locally. Its 2016 guide’s ten files are listed as pointers because the original PDF links redirected to the reading-room homepage during this pass; their contents have **not** been ingested.

## Evidence standard

- **A — verified record or archive metadata:** what a document says, who authored it, its date, page, custody, or recorded response.
- **B — sourced institutional interpretation:** an agency’s historical conclusion, which is evidence of its assessment, not a final answer to every UAP question.
- **C — uncorroborated allegation:** a claim preserved in a file without independent documentation here. Archive custody does not endorse it.

This page-by-page count explains the guide’s approximate extent, without claiming every physical or restricted Greer record is online. ## What the records establish

1. **Correspondence and access, A:** In the Alpha-file letter [NAID 300885568, p. 4](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/300885568), Greer says he and Laurance Rockefeller had worked on the issue and that he sent Rockefeller a packet to share with Clinton. This establishes what Greer wrote, not that Clinton received or read that packet, met Greer, or funded the project.
2. **NASA response, A for the letter; B for its 1996 position:** [NAID 300885580, pp. 2–5](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/300885580) contains a July 17, 1996 NASA reply saying Greer’s May 23 letter to President Clinton was referred to NASA. It says no factual evidence then established alien life elsewhere or a UFO–alien link. The enclosed 1996 fact sheet gives Project Blue Book’s 12,618 reports and 701 then categorized as unidentified, while saying that category did not establish extraterrestrial vehicles. “Referred” does not prove Clinton personally read or directed the response; NASA’s 1996 position is historical, not current UAP policy.
3. **Meeting requests and replies, A:** [NAID 300885582, pp. 3–5 and 40–41](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/300885582) shows Greer’s 1996 request and a December 6 White House scheduling decline. [NAID 300885583, pp. 2–3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/300885583) shows another request and a February 25, 1997 decline. These files provide no evidence of the requested presidential meeting.
4. **Disclosure allegations, C:** Greer’s Nov. 15, 1996 letter and attachments [NAID 300885582, pp. 4–38](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/300885582) allege covert projects, recovered extraterrestrial devices, private-sector control, witnesses, and cooperation across governments. Their presence in a presidential archive proves preservation of his submissions; the downloaded packet does not independently substantiate those assertions.

## CIA disclosure research

[[2026-09-25 - CIA role in UFO studies 1947 to 1990|Gerald K. Haines’s CIA historical study]] says the Agency sponsored the 1953 Robertson Panel and concealed its sponsorship for years; early analysis centered on air defense, public-report overload, and foreign exploitation. It reports a 1977–78 FOIA search finding 355 records, about 900 pages, and a December 1978 release of all but 57 records, about 100 pages, at that time. It describes a limited post-1953 CIA UFO role and no proof of extraterrestrial visitation in the reviewed files. This is **B**, an agency retrospective with citations, not a complete inventory of everything ever held or a warrant to dismiss new evidence.

[[2026-09-25 - CIA curated UFO document guide|CIA’s 2016 public guide]] points to ten 1949–53 era reports and memoranda, arranged to illustrate how differently the same topic can be read. It is an **A** record of what CIA selected for public attention, not evidence that all ten underlying files were successfully retrieved here. The guide’s ten legacy PDF URLs redirected to the reading-room home page on 2026-09-25; therefore the knowledge base contains their names and original pointers, and the locally ingested CIA text is the historical study. [CIA’s reading room](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/) remains the broad disclosure source; this project makes no “all CIA UFO files” claim.

## Ownership, money, and cross-border links

- **Record ownership/custody, A:** Greer/CSETI authored the advocacy papers; NASA and White House staff authored the institutional responses. The Clinton Presidential Library and NARA administer the presidential records under the Presidential Records Act, as the finding aid states.
- **Funding, unknown:** Greer reports working with Laurance Rockefeller. These files do not establish that Rockefeller financed CSETI, who funded Project Starlight, or any payment path to officials, defense contractors, or media.
- **Cross-border activity, C/unknown:** Greer describes intended coordination with the United Nations, foreign governments, and witnesses in Europe and Russia. The packet does not document actual cross-border funding or corroborate the claimed programs.
