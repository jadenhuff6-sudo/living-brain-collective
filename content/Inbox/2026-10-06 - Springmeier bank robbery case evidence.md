# Springmeier bank robbery case: what the evidence was

**Short answer:** the evidence the court rulings name against Springmeier is one witness: accomplice Anthony Huntington, who testified that Springmeier helped plan the robbery, the bomb diversion, the gun and the getaway car. Huntington was a co-conspirator facing his own charges. The gunman, Forrest Bateman, did not testify. The bomb charge never reached the jury because the judge found the evidence did not support it. The rulings do not describe any physical evidence linking Springmeier to the bank, but they do not transcribe the trial either, so that is "not described," not "proven absent."

## The crime
- 8 Oct 1997, about 1 p.m., Key Bank, Damascus, Oregon. A man in battle fatigues with an "FBI" tag duct-taped to the back of his jacket fired an assault rifle once into the ceiling and left with about $6,000. About 10 minutes earlier a bomb went off at Fantasyland Adult Video, 16014 SE 82nd Dr, six miles west; no one hurt. Officials called it a diversion. (Oregonian, 13 Feb 2002)
- Court records treat Bateman as the gunman. The 2002 indictment itself did not say which man robbed the bank (Oregonian).

## Charges
- Federal grand jury indictment of Springmeier (then 46) and Bateman (30): 29 Jan 2002 per the Oregonian (Wikipedia says 31 Jan). Case D. Or. CR 02-24-RE.
- Counts: (1) armed bank robbery; (2) using and carrying a firearm, a semi-automatic rifle, during it; (3) using and carrying a destructive device (the bomb). The government charged Springmeier as a helper (aider and abettor) on counts 1 and 2.
- Separately indicted for growing marijuana at his Corbett home after a March 2001 raid, in which police seized growing equipment, "several weapons" and white separatist literature (Oregonian). Arrested 1 Feb 2002, released to house arrest 4 Feb.

## What the jury heard (from the court rulings)
- **Anthony Huntington** testified that Springmeier worked with him and Bateman on "the general and detailed planning": choosing the bank, timing, entry and exit routes, the diversion device and its timing, the use of the gun, and getting a stolen car. That is direct testimony, and it is the only evidence of Springmeier's role the judge spells out.
- **Bateman did not testify.** The 2006 ruling says he "was not available as a witness," so some of his out-of-court statements came in as statements against his own interest. The rulings do not say what those statements were or whether they named Springmeier.
- **Springmeier's own lawyers stipulated that Bateman robbed the bank.** Their strategy: admit the robbery happened, deny Springmeier helped.
- A bank employee testified the bank was federally insured (a legal requirement for the charge, not evidence about Springmeier).
- In closing, the prosecutor suggested Springmeier had told his lawyer details only a participant would know. The defense did not object; the appeals court found it was not serious enough to reverse. It did not say the remark was proper.

## Verdict and sentence
- 12 Feb 2003: guilty of armed robbery and of helping with the gun. The jury also found unarmed robbery, which merged. The bomb count had already been kept from the jury for lack of supporting evidence.
- 14 Nov 2003: 51 months (top of the 41 to 51 month range) plus a mandatory 60 months for the gun, back to back: **111 months**, about 9 years 3 months. No sentence enhancements. Wikipedia adds a $7,500 fine and $6,488 restitution (not confirmed in a court record).
- Released around March 2011 after a halfway house stint (Wikipedia and supporters; not checked against prison records).

## Appeals
- Trial judge James Redden refused to acquit, 21 Mar 2003: a rational jury could find he helped plan, prepare and carry out the robbery including the gun.
- Ninth Circuit affirmed, unpublished memo dated 14 Oct 2004 on the reporter copy (Redden's 2006 order says 8 Nov 2004): enough evidence he "knew before the crime that a gun would be used and acted to facilitate or encourage that use."
- Supreme Court declined to hear it, 18 Jan 2005; rehearing denied 28 Mar 2005. That is not a review of the evidence.
- Habeas challenge denied 1 Mar 2006: most claims were never raised at trial or on appeal, and the judge found them meritless anyway.
- So the evidence itself was weighed twice (trial judge, Ninth Circuit), both on the standard of "could any reasonable jury convict," which is very deferential.

## The cooperator's deal (no court record found)
- Henry Makow (2011, supporter): Huntington got no time for the robbery and "over 120 years" of drug and gun exposure was forgiven.
- Patricia Springmeier (his wife, 2011 rebuttal, as reported by the Grok reviewer, not opened here): Huntington faced about 120 years and got 3.5 years.
- Pastor John Torell (2003, hostile to Springmeier): the cooperator, whom he calls "Roger," got 47 months on gun and marijuana charges after a promised deal fell through.
- These conflict. What is consistent: the key witness was an accomplice with strong reason to cooperate.

## His framing claim (supporters' version)
Henry Makow, 2011, reposted 2024: "no evidence"; he only knew Bateman from a Bible study; he was at a print shop in Eagle Creek but the owner refused to give an alibi; his lawyer Pat Birmingham sold him out; "Ann J Brown," the first federal judge, said "There is no evidence against this man." **None of this appears in the court rulings.** No record found that Judge Anna J. Brown sat on the case. Makow's own pieces contradict each other on who testified (one says Bateman), and he dates the robbery 6 Oct, not 8 Oct. Recent X posts (2025 and 2026, checked by Grok) repeat "framed" or "zero evidence" with no new documents.

## The other side (also not trial evidence)
- Oregonian 2002: in Feb 2001 Bateman was one of three people arrested at a house in Sandy where police found explosives, machine guns and white supremacist literature. Police said the two men met at a Christian Patriot Association meeting (his wife later said a Bible study) and grew marijuana for income.
- Torell (EAEC, Sept 2003), citing a local investigator's copies of search warrants: machine guns, a grenade launcher, explosives, and Army of God and "Bull Run Militia" material at the two men's homes. Secondhand. Torell also says Bateman agreed to testify and the cooperator was not used, which **contradicts the court record** (Huntington testified; Bateman did not).
- None of the weapons evidence is described in the rulings as trial evidence against Springmeier.

## Bottom line
The case came down to one question: do you believe an accomplice with a reason to deal? The jury did, and two courts said a reasonable jury could. "Sketchy" is a fair read in one specific sense: the rulings name no physical evidence and only one witness to his role, and the bomb charge was thrown out. "Framed with zero evidence" overstates it: a participant testified to his role in detail, and his own defense conceded the robbery and fought only his involvement. To settle it you would need the trial transcript and Huntington's plea deal, which are in the paid federal court records system (PACER) or the court's archives. That needs the founder's OK to spend.
